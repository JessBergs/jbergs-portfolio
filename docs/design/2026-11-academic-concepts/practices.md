# What makes a personal page read as academic

Distilled from 146 researcher homepages (the thirty in `../2026-11-reference-profiles/`
and the 116 high-profile pages catalogued there). These are the conventions the
concepts in this folder follow; the ugliness of most of the source pages is not one of
them.

## Order of the page

1. Photo and contacts
2. One-line statement of the research, then a short first-person bio
3. Upcoming talks and events
4. Projects (papers, studies, built things)
5. Past talks and conferences
6. Mentoring and service, then a last-updated line

The same order top to bottom on every concept. That is the convention: nothing is
hidden behind a tab or a click, and a reader scrolls once.

## The identity block

- The photo is small, square or round, beside or above the name. Never a hero image.
- The name is set in text, not display type. One or two lines of title and affiliation
  under it, the institution linked.
- Contacts are a short row of plain links: email, Scholar, CV or LinkedIn, and one
  invitation (office hours, coffee chat). Icons optional and tiny.

## The bio

- First person. The first sentence says what the research is about in plain words.
- Two or three short paragraphs. Institutions, programmes and collaborators are links
  in the sentences, not a separate list.
- A status note is its own short paragraph, set apart: what is happening this term,
  whether you are taking students or mentees, how to get a slot. It is dated or reads as
  dated ("this winter").

## Dates and lists

- Everything with a date is a list in reverse chronological order, with the date in a
  quiet gutter on the left: smaller, grey, fixed width, so the eye can run down it.
- An entry is one or two lines: title, then role and venue and place. Role words are the
  genre's vocabulary: Talk, Panel, Keynote, Workshop, Paper, Preprint, Study, Host,
  Mentor, Judge.
- Upcoming is the same list shape with future dates, kept short, with "to be confirmed"
  where that is true. It sits straight after the bio.
- Projects and papers get a small thumbnail on the left (the Barron list). Talks and
  conferences get a smaller one or none. Images are never bigger than a stamp.
- Venue and year are set in a second colour so the list scans; a one-line summary
  under the title says what was found or built.
- "Selected" with an "everything" link beats a complete list on the front page.

## Type and colour

- One typeface family, or one for text and one for small metadata. Body around 17px,
  measure under 75 characters.
- Section headings are sentence case, a step larger than body, bold or with a thin rule.
  No eyebrow labels.
- One link colour. Second colour only for the venue/type labels. Everything else ink and
  two greys.
- No cards with shadows, no gradients, no page-load motion.

## The last line

"Last updated November 2026." It tells the reader the page is kept.
